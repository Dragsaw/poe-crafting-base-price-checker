## Design Specificity Verdict

Three of the four are visibly derived from the page's own rules rather than bolted on: the unit glyph funded out of a retired tag rather than a reopened column, the equal-height control group preserving the threshold's established outer edge, Craft Cost placed where it is true instead of repeated twenty times. The recipe control's *conception* is specific to this page — the word is the control, derived from "the figure is the input". Its *resting state* is not: it has the exact anatomy of a static attribution block, which is literally what it was at revision 2. The component moved and its resting appearance did not.

**Deterministic scan.** 160 findings on the resting page, 192 on the expanded states. ## Overall Impression

The information architecture of this revision is genuinely good and the typography of its newest component is not. Craft Cost printed once on the control it belongs to, the refusal to invent a display name, the glyph funded out of a retirement — these are decisions a careful system makes. Then the control that carries them announces itself, at rest, as a caption.

**The single biggest opportunity: give the recipe control the page's own resting affordance.** It is three token changes, adds no chrome, and converts the revision's headline feature from *probably undiscoverable* to *obvious*.

## What's Working

1. **Craft Cost printed exactly once, on the control, never as a column.** Argued from the mechanism — `core` subtracts it once per Item Class, it is identical down every crafted row — not from taste. The alternative most teams ship would have broken a verified column sum and added twenty repetitions of an invariant to a page whose whole thesis is that noise hides the six rows that matter.

2. **`greater` / `perfect`: the refusal to invent a display name.** `recipes.json` declares no display string, so rather than coining one the control prints the single word that is the entire difference between the compositions, lifted from language the player already reads in-game — and states its own expiry condition. Best decision in the revision; I would not change a character.

3. **The glyph funded out of the retired `RAW BASE` tag, cue count held at three.** Explicit cue accounting, colour-alone ruled out on the page's own NFR-10, raw rows *gained* ~62px of name width while crafted rows gave up ~10px, and the "crafted is the unmarked default" asymmetry disappeared — which is what FR-3 actually requires. Sound even where the chosen characters are wrong.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The recipe control carries no resting affordance, on a page read without a pointer

**Why it matters.** DESIGN.md declares dotted sepia "the page's one vocabulary for *this is clickable*" twice, and says of the threshold that the resting dotted rule "is what makes it read as editable rather than as a label." The recipe's inactive option takes that rule **on hover only**. The stated usage scene is a page glanced at from across a desk with the pointer in the game — an affordance that exists only under the pointer does not exist in the scene the page was designed for. Compounding it, the inactive option is set in `ink-tertiary`, the ink reserved for labels and chrome, so the unchosen option reads as *disabled* rather than *available*.

The failure is silent: the page works fine on the default recipe, so nothing ever reveals that the second ranking dial was never found. **Alex is the worst case, not the best** — he formed his mental model at revision 2, when the composition sat in the eyebrow as attribution, and revision 3 put a panel there that still looks like attribution.

**Fix.** Three tokens, no arithmetic reopened, no chrome added:
- `inactiveRestingRule: '{spacing.hairline} dotted {colors.sepia}'` — **on the inactive option only.** A dotted rule on the active word would be a lie, since it is not a click target. Dotted = you can click this; solid = current value. On hover the inactive word's dotted rule promotes to solid sepia, which is exactly what `{components.expand-affordance}` already does.
- `inactiveColor` → `{colors.ink-secondary}`. An unchosen option is not chrome.
- `activeRule` → `2px solid {colors.sepia}`, so *active* is distinguishable from the 1px solid sepia that already means *hovered* on the sibling panel 16px away.

### [P1] The unit glyph pair discriminates on size — the one axis this system bans

**Where A and B disagreed, and what is actually true.** Assessment A argued from estimated metrics that `≡` is ~7×5px and collapses under blur into `▪`'s ~4×4px square. Assessment B measured the real Segoe UI outlines: `≡` ink **4.59 × 4.77px**, `▪` ink **2.26 × 2.27px** — a **4.3× ink-area** and **1.93× advance** difference. So A's pixel estimates were wrong and the pair is more distinguishable than A claimed.

**A's conclusion survives its own bad numbers, on the better argument.** `≡` is three ~1px strokes with ~0.9px gaps inside 4.77px of height; those gaps are the first thing an angled glance across a desk destroys, and once they fill, what remains is a 4.6px blob against a 2.3px blob. That is a **size** contrast — and "rank emphasis by weight, never by size" is a hard rule of this page. The glyphs quietly reintroduce size as a load-bearing discriminator.

**Fix.** `{components.unit-glyph-class}.glyph`: `≡` → **`§`** (U+00A7), keeping `▪`. `§` is a native reference-book mark in the register the page claims, carries a better mnemonic (a section contains numbered entries; a class contains Base Types), and shares no silhouette with anything in the existing vocabulary — tall, curved, two-lobed, vertically oriented. Under blur it collapses to a tall narrow vertical blob against `▪`'s small centred square: different height, aspect ratio **and line-box position**. B confirms U+00A7 is Latin-1 and present in Segoe UI, so no fallback. Cost, stated honestly: a faint legal/citation connotation, and it is letterlike on a page that prints no other letterlike symbols.

Also raise `{typography.row-unit-glyph.fontSize}` 10px → **11.5px**. It is the smallest mark on a row with ~10px of headroom, and it is the only cue for a distinction FR-3 requires on every row.

### [P1] Solid sepia means "active" here and "hover" 16px away — and sepia has started marking a state

DESIGN.md's Do's/Don'ts lists "Let sepia start meaning a state" as a Don't on the *same table row* that lists "the active recipe's rule" as a Do. A mark distinguishing selected from unselected is a state marker. The spine contradicts itself in one row.

**Why it matters beyond tidiness.** The sepia rule is the firewall protecting the two semantic inks — it is what lets sepia sit on the eyebrow, the threshold fill, every affordance and all twenty unit glyphs without competing with ochre and rust. Once sepia carries "selected", the rule is a convention rather than a law, and `pinned` and the curation fallback — both currently blocked from taking a colour and told to find a non-colour cue — have a precedent to cite.

**Fix.** Two parts. Take `activeRule` to 2px (which also resolves the P0 collision), and write the distinction the rule actually needs: **sepia may mark what the operator has chosen; it may never mark what the data is.** That keeps the firewall around the epistemic inks, licenses both the active rule and the threshold readout fill under one principle, and gives `pinned` a test to fail rather than a precedent to cite — a Curation Status is data, not an operator choice.

### [P2] The glyph leaves a ragged left edge down the page's primary scan column

**Found by B, missed by A, and it is mine.** `.bs .g` is `flex: 0 0 auto` with `margin-right: 7px` and **no fixed width**, so the name starts at **13.84px** on a crafted row and **10.54px** on a raw row — a 3.3px ragged left edge down the twenty-row column that is the page's main scan target, plus a 3.3px difference in usable name width before ellipsis. A separately noted that the names now start ~17px inside a header (`.c2`) that starts at 0, so the header no longer sits over its own content.

**Fix (applying).** Give the glyph a fixed-width box so both branches align exactly. That removes the raggedness deterministically.

**Not applying blind.** A's more elegant proposal — hang the glyph with a negative left margin into the rank column's 10px of padding, so names return flush to the column edge and the glyphs form their own column — is the more print-native answer and I would like it. But it changes visual alignment in a way **no browser exists in this session to verify**, and it is the kind of change that looks right in arithmetic and wrong on screen. Recommending it, not shipping it unseen.

### [P2] Craft Cost, the figure that validates every EV on the page, is its smallest type

Both assessments landed here independently — A from hierarchy, the detector from `undersized-ui-text`, which the exemption list does not cover. `{typography.recipe-cost}` is 9.5px sans, the size otherwise reserved for tracked uppercase labels and the threshold's range endpoints. The threshold figure beside it is 32px serif. It is less legible than a rank numeral.

`core` subtracts Craft Cost once per Item Class; it is the constant under every crafted EV. If it is wrong, stale or uncostable, the entire crafted branch is wrong — and this is deliberately the page's *only* printing of it. The placement is right and the type is inverted against its importance.

**Fix.** Set the figure in the serif at 13px with `Divine / craft` remaining 9.5px sans — reusing `{components.payout-threshold}`'s own figure-plus-quiet-unit anatomy, which also strengthens the sibling reading the control's discoverability depends on. Costs no width and no height: B measured **20.85px of dead interior space** in the panel, which `align-items: stretch` and `margin-top: auto` are currently spending on a void. The `no figure yet` fallback keeps its italic sans treatment; serif figure against italic sans phrase is itself informative.

### [P3] `·` joins in every chase cell and divides in the recipe

`T1 Cold Res · T1 Mana` means *this **and** that*. `greater · perfect` means *this **or** that, exclusively*. Same glyph, same ink, opposite operator. At an angled glance `greater · perfect` is structurally identical to a chase cell — two short words joined by a mid dot — which nudges the panel further toward reading as printed content. **Fix:** the pipe in `ink-tertiary` that `{components.trust-strip}` already uses to separate independent facts. The page owns a divider; it is using its joiner.

## Out of scope, found anyway, and worth a decision

**The incumbent glyph vocabulary is two typefaces, not one.** B read the font binaries: `◇` `↻` `✕` `△` `▸` `▾` are **absent from Segoe UI** and fall through to Segoe UI Symbol — a different face with ~8.61px advances against Segoe UI's ~6px. `●` `○` `↗` `†` are present. DESIGN.md claims a trust mark "takes the type size of the line it sits in… one vocabulary rendered at four line sizes, not four vocabularies." Typographically that is false today. NFR-7 still holds — Symbol is system-resident and nothing downloads — but the consistency claim does not. Within Segoe UI, `●` (4.30px ink) and `○` (7.25px ink) are not mass-matched either.

This predates revision 3 and touches five marks across the whole page, so I am reporting it rather than fixing it inside a scoped polish. It does mean the two *new* glyphs are better behaved than the vocabulary they were written to join.

**HTML defects I introduced.** `<a>` with no `href` ×8 (the trade-link cells) — an anchor without href is a placeholder, not a link. And `<i style="font-style:italic">` in the Raw Base panel title, where the inline style restates the element's own default. Both are mine, both trivial, both worth fixing. Pre-existing and not mine: `<n>` as a custom element ×15 in the sync report (invalid — custom element names need a hyphen), `<i>`/`<b>` as empty track boxes, `<u>` as an affordance hook, and no `h1` in the expanded-states file.

**Clean under independent check:** all seven column sums (1012, 966, 970) verified from the CSS with comments stripped; zero unused selectors; zero undefined classes; no unclosed tags, no duplicate IDs, no block-in-inline. The masthead group renders 216+16+276 = 508 with both panels at 109.4px, so `controlPanelsEqualHeight` does what it claims.

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan the first-timer — not applicable, and saying so is the finding.** One user, who authored the weights data, curated the Tracked List and wrote the Accepted Tiers. Any finding premised on "a new user wouldn't know X" is invalid here, and the decisions to drop the `RAW BASE` word and withhold a key-block entry are *correct* on exactly that ground.

**Alex the power user — primary, and the P0 hurts him most.** His mental model formed at revision 2, when the recipe sat in the eyebrow as attribution. Revision 3 moved it into a panel that at rest still looks like attribution. An expert's established model is the strongest force working against this control's discovery, and nothing at rest overturns it. Second flag: **he cannot do the comparison the control exists for.** The real question is *which recipe should I run tonight?*, and answering it means flipping between two full reorderings while memorising the top five — a working-memory exercise, with the one figure that could be compared without flipping set at 9.5px.

**Riley the stress tester — three.** `optionTextLimit` is load-bearing and has no failure treatment: a third recipe, or a second that doesn't reduce to one word, leaves the control with no defined rendering in a panel already holding 115px of words. The uncostable state has an undefined blast radius — the spine says outright what the crafted rows do there is unspecified, so the control can drive the page into a state the design has not defined. And nobody has measured the unit column against real data: the mockup uses hand-picked short names, and no one has checked the longest real Item Class or Base Type against 222px less the glyph's lead.

## Minor Observations

- The resting mockup shows `perfect` active, putting the sepia rule under the **right-hand** word of a pair, where it reads more like typographic emphasis than selection. The less favourable of the two states, shown incidentally rather than chosen.
- The unit glyph mapping could be taught for free in `{components.running-foot}`, which already carries explanatory prose. One clause costs no budget, adds no key-block column, and does not contradict the memlog 184 decision — the key block disambiguates empty cells; the foot explains the page.
- Putting the glyphs inline in the column header was costed and rejected: ~34px added to a ~185px label against 214px available fits only if tracking drops to ~0.12em, which would create a fifth tracking size and break the "uppercase at exactly four sizes" rule.
- `optionSeparator` is specified as a string with a leading space and an embedded prose clause, unlike every neighbouring token. A builder will guess.
- `readoutInteractive: 'n/a — this panel has no readout'` is a token whose value explains its own absence. Keep it; it stops a builder adding a track.
- The detector flagged `kicker-above-heading` on the masthead eyebrow, independently matching the craft floor's standing ban. Overridden by the committed world, which names the eyebrow as part of the reference-book register — but revision 3 *changed* that line, which was the moment to re-examine it, and I did not.

## Questions to Consider

1. The threshold announces itself at rest and the recipe does not. Was that decided, or did the recipe inherit a hover-only affordance from the segmented control that was rejected — where the affordance lived in a track that is present at rest? Stripping the chrome removed the chrome *and* the resting affordance, and only the chrome was meant to go.
2. The spine says a unit glyph needs no word "because a unit can be read off the name beside it." Can it? `Ring` is a class and `Sapphire Ring` is a base; `Amulet` and `Stellar Amulet` likewise. The distinguishing feature is a prefix word at 14px read at an angle. If the name settles it, the glyph is redundant and FR-3 is met by the name alone. If it does not, the glyph is load-bearing and withholding a legend needs a better argument than it currently has.
3. Is the operator's question "rank under the perfect recipe" or "which recipe should I run"? The control answers the first; everything about its use points at the second. If it is the second, the most valuable thing on the page is the *difference* between two rankings, and the design has never been asked to render a difference.
4. The masthead holds two dials, treats them as peers, and renders one at 32px and one at 15px. If the recipe is the more consequential dial — it changes which outcomes are reachable, not merely what an attempt costs — the hierarchy is inverted.
5. Sepia means nothing, except it now means "chosen". `pinned` and the curation fallback are both blocked from semantic inks and told to find a non-colour cue. Sepia is not a semantic ink. What stops them landing there?
