# PRD Quality Review — PoE2 Crafting Base Price Checker (revision 2)

*Reviewed against `.claude/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`. Scope: `prd.md` + `addendum.md`. Stakes: hobby/solo, single user; shape: chain-top capability spec downstream of a settled architecture spine.*

## Overall verdict

This is a genuinely good PRD and an unusually honest one: the thesis (threshold-truncated EV replaces memorisation) is stated, bet on, and visible in every feature; the trade-offs are named with what was given up; and the three previously-blocking defects are resolved in the spine rather than papered over, with the reasoning retained at §10 rather than deleted. The valuation chain — FR-1, FR-16, FR-21, FR-22, FR-29 — is specified to a level where two independent implementers should converge, which is the hardest thing this document had to do and it does it.

What is at risk is the *sync scheduling* chain, not the valuation chain. FR-17's Refresh Rotation silently mixes two different enums (Curation Status and Price State) with no precedence rule, its `unresolvable` retry rank has nothing left to retry given FR-24's pre-request detection, and its 25%-pinned cap is denominated against a "search ceiling" that is a daily figure applied to a per-Chunk decision — while the Chunk's own allowance is never quantified anywhere in the document. Separately, FR-3's Raw Bases need no weights at all, yet FR-4/FR-28/FR-30 sweep them into Unrankable, which understates what exists on day one and misstates the §7.3 release gate.

Verdict: **buildable, with a scheduling layer that will be resolved by whoever writes the story rather than by this document.** Five high findings, all localised, none requiring a structural change.

## Decision-readiness — strong

A decision-maker can act on this. Decisions are stated as decisions and the losers are named: §7.2 rejects a second Craft Recipe with the reason ("a cost column presented as an analysis" — addendum), rejects authenticated sync while quantifying what it would buy ("Revisit only if a full refresh must exceed ~1,500 searches"), and holds coarser fallback pricing as an explicitly named option with a trigger condition rather than a vague "maybe later". §6's loot-filter non-goal does the harder thing and states that the brief's own stated outcome — *"eventually I won't have to memorize anything"* — **"is not achievable"**. That is a PRD contradicting its own source document to the user's face, which is what decision-readiness actually looks like.

The Open Questions pass the "actually open" test. OQ-5, OQ-6 and OQ-7 have no answer in the next sentence; OQ-7 in particular concedes a circularity it cannot escape ("The first Tracked List must be authored from the player's existing knowledge — the very knowledge the tool exists to supply") and calls it "the acknowledged price of the curation approach" rather than inventing a mechanism. §10's *Resolved by spine rev 2* block is correctly framed — the defects are labelled as defects in the **inherited** model, the resolution is cited to specific ADs, and the retained analysis is justified ("a reader who wants to know why a Modifier Reference must carry a ceiling ... will find the argument only here"). Retaining it is the right call and costs nothing, since §0 tells the reader up front that "Nothing in §4 is gated on a pending amendment."

Risks are not smoothed. R-1 says the mitigation is "honesty only (FR-13), not correction", and FR-13 repeats it against itself: *"This is the only mitigation in the system for Risk R-1 ... and a copywriting rule does not replace it."* R-3 turns the honesty apparatus on its own best input — `measured` "means *measured by someone*, not *ground truth*". A PRD that undercuts its own quality signal in its own risk list is not smoothing anything to neutral.

The two `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit at real tensions (FR-18's "it does not close it"; §7.2's coarser-fallback trigger), not at safe checkpoints.

### Findings

- **medium** §7.3's dependency is accepted on an unverifiable claim (§7.3) — "The project is near completion" is the only evidence offered for the single dependency that gates the entire release, with no owner, no date, and no statement of what happens to this build if it slips. The rest of §7.3 is precise about *consequence* (every Base Type Unrankable) but silent on *contingency*. For a solo project where the author owns both sides this may genuinely be fine — but the PRD should say that it is fine, rather than leave the reader to infer it. *Fix:* one sentence naming the producer, the expected date, and the decision if it slips (wait / ship the Unrankable-only view / ship a uniform-prior file with sourced pools per FR-30).

## Substance over theater — strong

There is no persona section, and that is correct rather than an omission: §2.2 defines the user negatively ("Anyone who is not the author") and §2.1's Jobs To Be Done each drive a specific requirement — "Know when the answer has gone stale" produces §4.4 wholesale, "Spend time playing, not crafting" produces the Payout Threshold's truncation semantics, "Keep a tool alive for a year" produces NFR-7 and SM-5. Zero furniture personas.

The NFRs are not boilerplate. NFR-6 carries a number (100 ms) *and* the forbidden escape hatch ("the fix is memoising the pure function — never precomputing in sync"). NFR-1 explains why it is the hardest requirement in terms specific to this product: "what makes a red test mean 'the code is wrong' rather than 'GGG was slow.'" NFR-10 is the one that earns the most credit — it declares that it *extends* AD-24 beyond the spine's literal scope and tags that extension as this PRD's own assumption, which is the opposite of NFR theater.

The Vision cannot be swapped into another PRD: "That apprenticeship is destroyed three or four times a year by league resets, at precisely the moment it is worth the most," followed by the distinction the whole system exists to hold — "A base with one jackpot combination and a base with many moderate ones swap places as the threshold moves, which is why the threshold is a dial the player turns rather than a constant someone compiled in."

No findings.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is explicit and the features are derived from it rather than collected. The chain is traceable: threshold-truncated EV is the bet → therefore the threshold must be a live control (FR-6) → therefore nothing in the Dataset may carry a rank, which FR-1's feature description states as a consequence rather than a preference ("a precomputed ranking would make the Payout Threshold reorder nothing until the next sync, which would make the product's central control a lie") → therefore ranking runs in the browser under a 100 ms budget (NFR-6).

Success Metrics validate the thesis rather than measuring activity, and the PRD is candid that the instrument is the player's own recollection because "instrumenting the tool would be more work than the signal is worth." SM-2 ("The mental top-five goes away") is a direct test of the memorisation thesis. Counter-metrics are present and each names the specific failure it guards: SM-C1 is quantified and carries the reason it is denominated in *searches* rather than entries; SM-C4 ("Apparent confidence") names the exact way this product would become dangerous. The counter-metric-to-metric pairings are stated ("Counterbalances SM-4"), which most PRDs skip.

MVP scope kind is coherently problem-solving, and §7.2's de-scoping logic matches — a second recipe is cut *because* AD-18 makes ordering recipe-invariant, not because it is expensive.

### Findings

- **medium** SM-3 is the only metric with no requirement that plausibly delivers it (§8) — "A league start costs days, not weeks. ... Validates FR-31, FR-32." FR-31 and FR-32 deliver an *honest empty state*, not a fast refill. What actually determines whether the list is useful "within days" is refresh throughput — §4.6's "roughly fifteen hours" for a full pass — and the Refresh Rotation of FR-17, neither of which SM-3 cites. As written, a league start could take the full rotation period and both cited FRs would still pass. *Fix:* cite FR-17 and §4.6's refresh duration in SM-3, or restate SM-3 as "the ranking is visibly empty rather than wrong, and refills within one full rotation."

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

The valuation requirements are the strongest part of the document and would survive a hostile implementer. FR-29 is the model: it states the containment rule with the comparison operators spelled out (`band.valueMin >=` ... `band.valueMax <=`), names the failure mode it is defending against ("The `(Base Type, slot)` scoping is not optional and is the commonest way two implementations diverge"), and specifies that an empty containment set is **"a validation error, not a `P = 0`"** with the reason. FR-21 does the same for pricing, down to the detail that instant buyout is the option "whose `id` is JSON `null` and must be emitted explicitly rather than dropped by a serialiser" and is **not** `priced_with_info` — with the consequence of getting it wrong stated ("it silently admits unpriced listings and corrupts every estimate"). FR-16's overlap rule replaces an enumeration with a predicate and explains why ("an enumerated list of shapes has now missed a case twice"), then works the three consequences including the one the enumeration missed. This is unusually good.

FR-4's coverage table is the right answer to a gate that would otherwise have been an adjective — three bands, a measured quantity, and a stated sequencing constraint ("Committing a layout before that number exists is committing to an assumption about how much of the product there is"). FR-11 and FR-12 are both testable and both name the thing they refuse to do: FR-11's banner "**disappears on its own** once any `measured` figure is present — it is a function of the data, not a build-time constant someone must remember to remove"; FR-12 refuses to collapse two clocks and explains the distinction with a worked contrast. FR-33's eight-artifact list is enumerated exactly, with the three deliberate exclusions named.

The weakness is concentrated in FR-17 and the Chunk budget around it. Three separate under-specifications compound: the rotation table mixes two enums without a precedence rule, rank 3 retries something FR-24 detects without a request, and the pinned cap is expressed as a percentage of a ceiling that is daily while the behaviour it bounds is per-Chunk. Underneath all three, the Chunk's own search and fetch allowances — the quantities FR-19 makes the Chunk's terminating condition — appear nowhere in the document as numbers. A builder can implement the valuation chain from this PRD; they cannot implement the scheduler from it without inventing three values.

### Findings

- **high** FR-17's rotation table mixes Curation Status and Price State with no precedence (§4.5 / FR-17) — ranks 1, 2 and 4 select on Curation Status (`pinned`, `active`, `pruned`); rank 3 selects on Price State (`unresolvable`). These are orthogonal per the Glossary: an entry is simultaneously `active` *and* `unresolvable`. Such an entry matches rank 2 (by oldest `lastAttemptedAt`) *and* rank 3 (bounded 24h retry), and the table gives no rule for which wins — so one implementation refreshes it every Chunk and another once a day, both conforming. The Glossary's Refresh Rotation entry repeats the same conflation verbatim. *Fix:* state that rank 3 is a *filter* applied over ranks 1–2, not a rank — e.g. "an entry whose Price State is `unresolvable` is skipped in ranks 1 and 2 unless 24h have elapsed since its `lastAttemptedAt`."
- **high** FR-17's `unresolvable` retry has nothing to retry (§4.5 / FR-17 vs §4.6 / FR-24) — FR-24 states detection is "by validating against the **committed Trade Catalogue** ... **before any request is issued** — never inferred from results." Against an unchanged committed catalogue, that check is deterministic and free: re-running it yields the same answer and consumes no budget. FR-17's rationale for rank 3 — "a patched-out modifier neither vanishes nor consumes budget every Chunk" — describes a cost that does not exist under FR-24, and the 24h bound therefore throttles nothing. The real trigger for re-evaluating an `unresolvable` entry is a *catalogue refresh* (FR-24, human-invoked at patch cadence), not elapsed time. *Fix:* replace the 24h bound with "re-evaluated when the Trade Catalogue changes", or state the case FR-24 does not cover (a search that resolves at validation but fails at the API) and scope rank 3 to it.
- **high** The Chunk's search and fetch allowances are never quantified (§4.6 / FR-19, §4.5 / FR-14) — FR-19 makes a Chunk end at "the remaining search allowance, the remaining fetch allowance, or the workload remainder", but neither allowance appears as a number or a derivation rule anywhere in the PRD. FR-14 gives only a *daily* figure (~1,500 searches against ~2,400), and §4.6 gives only a total duration ("roughly fifteen hours"). A builder must therefore invent the single number that determines how often the scheduler is invoked, how large a Chunk is, and — via the finding below — what the pinned cap means. FR-14's "The ceiling is denominated in searches, not entries" is precise about the *unit* and silent about the *window*. *Fix:* state the per-Chunk allowance, or state explicitly that it is derived at runtime from the live rate-limit bucket (FR-20) and is therefore not a PRD constant.
- **high** FR-17's 25% pinned cap is ambiguous between a per-Chunk and a per-day denominator (§4.5 / FR-17) — "The `pinned` set is capped at 25% of the search ceiling (FR-14), enforced as a `data/tracked.json` validation error." FR-14's ceiling is ~1,500 searches *per full refresh / per day*, so read literally the cap permits 375 pinned entries. But `pinned` entries refresh in **every Chunk**, and a fifteen-hour full pass implies many Chunks per day — so 375 pinned entries would consume the daily budget several times over, which is precisely the starvation failure the cap exists to prevent ("An oversized pinned set consumes every Chunk and starves both its own tail and the `active` rotation below it"). The cap is almost certainly meant as 25% of a *Chunk's* allowance, which the finding above shows is itself unstated. As written, the validation rule cannot be implemented correctly. *Fix:* denominate the cap against the per-Chunk search allowance explicitly, and give the validation error a computable form.
- **high** Raw Bases are swept into Unrankable despite needing no weights (§4.1 / FR-3 vs FR-4, FR-28, FR-30) — FR-3 defines a Raw Base's EV as "its observed price with zero Craft Cost", with no probability term and therefore no dependence on the Eligible Pool. Yet FR-4 makes any Base Type "absent from the Weights File entirely" Unrankable, and FR-30 states flatly "Until a conforming file exists, **every Base Type is Unrankable**." Both sweep Raw Bases in. This propagates into §7.3's release gate ("until a conforming file exists ... the product has no ranked list at all"), which is stronger than true — a Raw Base branch ranks perfectly well on price alone — and into FR-4's coverage measurement, whose denominator ("Base Types resolving to `complete` in **both** slots") will count Base Types tracked *only* as Raw Bases as failures, depressing the AD-27 gate figure that binds a layout decision. *Fix:* exempt Raw Bases from the pool-completeness requirement in FR-4 and FR-30 (as FR-16 already exempts them from the shared-floor rule, and for the same reason — "it is never a summand"), and exclude Raw-Base-only Base Types from FR-4's coverage denominator.
- **medium** FR-16's overlap predicate does not scope band intersection to a shared `statId` (§4.5 / FR-16) — "Two entries on one Base Type overlap when, **for both slots**, either the slot is absent in one of them or their bands intersect." The predicate is correct on every case the FR then works through, but "their bands intersect" compares value ranges without requiring the two Modifier References to name the same `statId`. Two prefix entries on different `statId`s with numerically overlapping value ranges (e.g. `[10,20]` on each) are mutually exclusive outcomes — an item has one prefix — yet a literal implementation of the predicate rejects them. Given that the FR's whole point is that the predicate replaces an enumeration that "has now missed a case twice", the predicate needs to be exact. *Fix:* "...or their Modifier References carry the same `statId` and their bands intersect."
- **medium** FR-33 requires a consistent artifact set but specifies no mechanism (§4.10 / FR-33) — "`web` renders from a **single consistent set** and does not mix artifacts across a refresh (AD-24)", stated alongside "fetches exactly eight artifacts at runtime as **separate cache-busted requests**". Eight independent requests racing a sync commit can straddle it; nothing in the FR says how the set is identified as one set (a manifest, a commit sha, a shared version stamp, a retry rule). This is the one consistency requirement in the document with no testable consequence attached to it. *Fix:* name the mechanism — e.g. all eight carry a `datasetVersion` and `web` refetches the set if they disagree.
- **medium** FR-21's median over an even sample is undefined (§4.6 / FR-21) — "the **median of those listings' prices after normalisation to Divine**" over "the cheapest 10 result ids". Two implementers will reasonably choose the mean of the 5th and 6th value versus the 5th or 6th alone, and the results differ on every skewed sample — which is every crafting-base sample, since the set is sorted ascending. The FR is otherwise precise to the level of serialiser behaviour, so this stands out. *Fix:* state the convention (lower median / arithmetic mean of the two central values) and how it behaves at sample sizes 1 and 2.
- **low** Negative-EV Base Types have no stated treatment in the ranked list (§4.1 / FR-1, FR-2, FR-5) — FR-2 establishes that a base with no above-threshold Combinations has an EV "negative by its Craft Cost", and FR-5 bounds the list to 20 by EV. Whether negative-EV rows appear at all, are filtered, or are visually marked is unstated; at a high threshold most of the tracked list is negative and the top-20 cut may itself fall below zero. *Fix:* one consequence on FR-5 stating whether the ordering is truncated at EV ≤ 0.

## Scope honesty — strong

§6 is doing real work rather than listing things nobody proposed. Each non-goal carries its reason and, where relevant, its cost: the loot-filter entry explains the technical cause ("PoE2's filter syntax has no working equivalent of `HasExplicitMod`"), states the permanent ceiling, and concludes that "the view [is] the permanent product rather than a stepping stone" — a strategic consequence, not a deferral. The augment non-goal admits the excluded decision is made "dozens of times a session — far more often than the ranked list is read", which is the uncomfortable version. "Price history features" names the exact downstream effect on UJ-5's judgment.

§7.2's out-of-scope items each carry a revisit trigger, and the weights entry does the rare thing of distinguishing two different scopes explicitly: "What is out of scope is this repo producing weights, not the app having good ones." The spawn-weight-disambiguation entry names its own opportunity cost ("the reason the weights deferral above has a cost beyond provenance labelling"), which is de-scoping proposed honestly rather than done silently.

Assumptions roundtrip cleanly: nine inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags across FR-2 (×2), FR-3, FR-5, FR-7, FR-18, FR-26, FR-31 and NFR-10, all present in §11, no orphan index entries. Each is a real inference, not a hedge — FR-3's even states the condition under which it breaks ("If bases above 82 exist, this needs a ceiling, not a floor").

Open-items density is appropriate: three resolved BQs marked resolved, four genuinely open OQs of which three are explicitly non-blocking and one (OQ-4) is closed and transferred with a named owner. For a hobby/solo green-light-to-build document, nine assumptions and four open questions is not a blocker.

## Downstream usability — adequate

This dimension carries real weight here: the PRD is chain-top (it feeds epics/stories) and §7.3 makes it half the contract for an external project. The FR/UJ/SM/NFR/R id spaces are contiguous and unique — FR-1 through FR-33 with no gaps or duplicates, UJ-1..6, SM-1..6 plus SM-C1..4, NFR-1..10, R-1..6 — and every cross-reference I followed resolves to an existing target. Sections are largely extractable alone: FR-29 can be handed to a developer with no other context and still specifies the derivation, because it repeats the band arithmetic rather than saying "see FR-27".

The Glossary is the document's spine and is mostly held to. Two things undercut it, one of them consequential for the external contract.

### Findings

- **medium** `itemLevelMin` names two different concepts (§3, FR-22, FR-27, FR-29) — on a Tracked Entry it is the **Item Level Floor** ("`itemLevelMin` is a declared field on the Tracked Entry, authored by hand", FR-22); on a Weights File band it is the item level at which that band *becomes available* ("`(statId, valueMin, valueMax, itemLevelMin, weight)`", FR-27). FR-29 uses both in one sentence — `band.itemLevelMin <= L`, where `L` is the entry's floor — so the two are compared but never distinguished by name. The Glossary defines Item Level Floor and never mentions that the field carrying it shares a name with a different field on a different artifact. This is exactly the kind of drift that produces a wrong join in a story implementation, and it lands on the schema the external project builds to. *Fix:* rename one side in the contract (e.g. band `availableFromItemLevel`), or add a Glossary note stating the collision explicitly and which artifact each spelling belongs to.
- **medium** The Glossary's **Workload** entry miscounts the request sources (§3) — "the set of Tracked Entries and currencies a Chunk may spend requests on, held in **two declared files**. It is **one of four** enumerated request sources; **the other two** — the per-run leagues check and the catalogue refresh — are fixed overheads." One-of-four leaves three others; two are then named. FR-14 has it right (the two workload files are two of the four sources, the leagues check and catalogue refresh are the other two), so the Glossary entry — the definition downstream readers are told to use verbatim — is the one that is wrong. *Fix:* "It comprises two of the four enumerated request sources; the other two ... are fixed overheads."
- **medium** §7.3 declares itself a contract but omits the contract's identifying details (§7.3) — FR-28 states "this document plus `WEIGHTS-FILE-SCHEMA.md` are its entire contract", and §7.3 is where an external reader would start. It never names the schema version the producer must target (**2.0.0**, breaking against 1.x — stated only in FR-27), the delivery filename (`weights.json` — appears only in FR-33's artifact list), or any executable validator the producer can check against before shipping. A producer reading §7.3 alone would ship to the wrong major. *Fix:* add the schema version, the expected artifact path, and a pointer to the validation command (or note that `core`'s load-time rejection is the only check) to §7.3's first bullet.
- **low** FR-16's enforcement owner is unnamed (§4.5 / FR-16, FR-17) — "Overlap is rejected **at load** with the offending entries named". Which load? `core` loads `tracked.json` in the browser (FR-33), `sync` also reads and validates it (FR-24, FR-17's pinned cap "enforced as a `data/tracked.json` validation error"). If browser-load is the only gate, a bad curation commit is discovered when the site breaks — after UJ-5's "commits, and the next sync run reflects it" has already happened. No pre-commit or CLI validation path is named anywhere in the PRD, and NFR-8's "The producer validates before writing" does not apply to a hand-authored file with no producer. *Fix:* name the validation surface for hand-owned files (a `pnpm validate` command, a CI check, or explicitly "both `sync` and `core` reject it, and the player finds out at the next sync").
- **low** FR-29's empty-containment-set error is not routed to any surface (§4.8 / FR-29, §4.6 / FR-25, FR-27) — it is declared "a **validation error**, not a `P = 0`", but it is a cross-artifact error (tracked list × Weights File) and appears in neither FR-27's hard-file-error list (which rejects the *file*, wrong remedy here) nor FR-25's Sync Report contents. Where the curator sees it is unstated. *Fix:* add it to the Sync Report's recorded contents in FR-25, alongside unresolvable entries.

## Shape fit — strong

The shape is right and the PRD says why it is right rather than defaulting into it. §2.3 opens with "Single operator, single role, no authentication and no multi-device handoff, so these are written in the template's lighter form" — six short UJs, each with the same protagonist carrying enough context inline to stand alone (UJ-4's "sees its price was observed three days ago and that the whole ranking is flagged as resting on the uniform prior, and discounts it"), and no UJ density theater. For a single-operator tool this is the correct amount: enough to anchor FRs (each feature names which UJs it realizes) and not so much that it pretends at multi-stakeholder complexity.

The capability-spec shape dominates §4, which is correct for this product, and §8's metrics are appropriately operational-and-behavioural rather than pretending at instrumentation the product will never have ("each metric is observed by the player's own recollection — the only instrument available"). Rigor is calibrated to hobby stakes — no compliance traceability, no rollout plan, no support model — while the substance bar is plainly met.

The one genuinely unusual shape choice, stating arithmetic inside FRs, is defended explicitly in the addendum's closing section: these are "not implementation details that happen to be decided; they are the behaviour the player is buying, and they are the places where two independently-built components could satisfy a looser requirement in mutually incompatible ways." Given that FR-1, FR-21 and FR-29 are consumed by different packages *and* by an external project, that is the right call, and the citation-not-restatement discipline keeps the PRD from becoming a second source of truth for the spine. The inheritance of AD-1..AD-27 is a recorded choice (§0), consistently executed, and is not a defect.
