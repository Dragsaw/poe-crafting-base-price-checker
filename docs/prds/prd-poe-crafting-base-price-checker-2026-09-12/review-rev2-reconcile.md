# Reconcile review — did the proposals land, and did they land faithfully?

## Verdict

**The update is faithful.** All 37 numbered obligations in the proposals document landed, and landed as *content*, not merely as deletions — every one I checked says what the proposal asked it to say, in the place the proposal named. No stale phrase survives in `prd.md` or `addendum.md` (`Depends on BQ-n`, `floor spanning tiers`, `nested value floors`, `No third exists`, `type_filters.category`, `priced_with_info`, `refreshed as part of the non-workload requests`, `generated by a one-off script`, `the maximum across that Base Type's entries`, `six artifacts`, and every RePoE premise are all gone; the only remaining hits in the directory are in the older `review-adversarial.md` / `reconcile-*.md` files, which are historical records and not targets).

C-1's negative requirement is satisfied: FR-3, FR-22 and the Glossary's *Raw Base* are all standing, and the only additions around them are the Raw Base floor exemption the spine's AD-17 rule 3 now grants — which is the decision C-1 records, not a contradiction of it.

What the update does not do is *close the seams it opened*. Eight findings below; two are high severity because a builder reading the PRD cannot act without guessing.

## 2. Intent missed despite changed wording

Only one, and it is the highest-severity finding in this review.

### F1 — High — C-12 removed the only rule for *how* a Base Type's single Item Level Floor is arrived at

C-12 called *"the maximum across that Base Type's entries"* vacuous *"once entries must share a floor"* and asked for a collapse to *"a Base Type has one Item Level Floor."* The PRD complied exactly. But that sentence was doing two jobs, and only one of them was vacuous.

The PRD now asserts a constraint with no reconciliation procedure behind it, and the two statements sit four bullets apart in the same FR:

> FR-22: *"A Tracked Entry's **Item Level Floor** is the highest item level among its affixes' Accepted Tiers."* — a **per-entry** derivation

> FR-22: *"`itemLevelMin` is a declared field on the Tracked Entry, authored by hand. **Neither `sync` nor `core` infers or adjusts it.**"*

> FR-22: *"**A Base Type has one Item Level Floor.** Its crafted entries share it, enforced at load (FR-16, AD-17)."*

Take the PRD's own worked example. A bow entry carrying crit-chance T1 (73) and phys-damage T2 (75) derives floor 75. A second bow entry carrying only crit-chance T1 derives floor 73. Both are legal single entries; together they violate the shared-floor rule and FR-16 rejects the tracked list at load. The PRD nowhere says what the curator does about it. The three available answers — raise every entry on the base to the maximum derived floor (the old sentence, which was *not* vacuous), drop the entry that disagrees, or split the base — have materially different consequences, because under FR-29 the floor is *"a valuation input, not only a search parameter"*: raising an entry's floor re-scopes its Eligible Pool and changes its probability. A builder writing the load-time validator has to choose whether it merely *rejects* or also *reports the floor to raise to*, and a curator seeding a list has no stated rule.

The other side of the same gap: FR-16 says *"Entries at floors 75 and 82 are nested, not disjoint"* and rejects them — so the rule is real and enforced — while FR-22 hands the curator a derivation that routinely produces exactly that. The proposals never intended to remove the reconciliation; C-12 was aimed at the redundant *display* clause (*"the per-Base-Type floor **visible to the player**"* in the original), and the collapse took the derivation with it.

**Fix shape:** restore one sentence to FR-22 — the Base Type's Item Level Floor is the **maximum** of its crafted entries' derived floors, and every crafted entry on it is authored at that floor; the per-affix rule derives a *candidate*, the maximum resolves it. That is compatible with C-12's actual complaint and with AD-17.

## 3. Self-contradictions introduced by the edits

### F2 — High — the 25% `pinned` cap is denominated against a quantity that cannot bound it

FR-17 states the cap the proposals asked for (AD-26: *"capped at 25% of the search ceiling"*) and, in the same bullet, states the harm it exists to prevent:

> FR-17: *"**The `pinned` set is capped at 25% of the search ceiling** (FR-14)… An oversized pinned set consumes every Chunk and starves both its own tail and the `active` rotation below it."*

But FR-14's ceiling is per **full refresh**, not per Chunk:

> FR-14: *"A full refresh is held to approximately **1,500 searches** against a measured ceiling of ~2,400 per day."*

> §4.6: *"A full refresh takes roughly fifteen hours and consumes about 62% of the daily search budget."*

A full refresh therefore spans many Chunks, and `pinned` entries are re-searched in **every** one of them (FR-17 row 1, FR-15). Read literally, the cap permits 375 pinned entries, each costing a search per Chunk. Across the Chunks of a single fifteen-hour pass that is multiples of the 1,500-search ceiling — the cap admits precisely the starvation its own justification describes. The two numbers are not commensurable: one is a per-pass total, the other is a per-Chunk recurring cost.

This matters to a builder because the PRD says the cap is **mechanically enforced** (*"enforced as a `data/tracked.json` validation error rather than a convention"*), so someone must write the inequality. As written there is no correct one. The cap needs a per-Chunk denominator (25% of a Chunk's search allowance) or a per-day one (25% of ~2,400 across the day's Chunks) — and the two give very different limits.

The proposals asked for this clause verbatim from AD-26, so the flaw is inherited rather than invented. But the PRD is where it becomes checkable, and it is not checkable here. Worth raising against the spine rather than picking a reading locally.

### F3 — Medium-high — FR-17 row 3 and FR-24 disagree about what an `unresolvable` retry costs and whether it can succeed

FR-24, rewritten by C-6, makes `unresolvable` a purely **local** determination:

> FR-24: *"Detection is by validating against the **committed Trade Catalogue** (`data/catalogue/*.json`), **before any request is issued** — never inferred from results."*

> FR-24: *"The catalogue is refreshed by an **explicit human-invoked command at GGG patch cadence**, never as part of a sync run (AD-25)."*

FR-17, written fresh from AD-26, budgets retries as though they were remote and could change outcome:

> FR-17 row 3: *"**`unresolvable`** entries on a bounded retry — at most one attempt per entry per 24h | a patched-out modifier neither vanishes nor **consumes budget** every Chunk (FR-24)"*

Under FR-24 an `unresolvable` entry's state cannot change until a human runs `pnpm catalogue:refresh`, and re-checking it costs zero trade requests. So either the 24h bound guards nothing (there is no budget to guard), or `sync` is issuing a live request for an id the committed catalogue says does not exist — which FR-24's *"before any request is issued"* forbids. Neither reading is stated, and the rationale column asserts the second.

There is also a straightforward citation error: row 3 cites **FR-24** for the bounded-retry rule, and FR-24 contains no retry rule of any kind. This is the same class of defect as carried-over items A-7, A-8 and A-10, all of which this update fixed.

**Fix shape:** say what a retry actually is — most likely *re-validation against the catalogue, at no request cost, whenever the catalogue's `schemaVersion`/fetch timestamp has changed* — and drop the budget rationale, or move the entry out of the request-ordered rotation entirely since it consumes no requests.

### F4 — Medium — `pinned` is defined three ways across the Glossary, FR-15 and FR-17

| Location | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| Glossary *Curation Status* | *"`pinned` (refreshed every Chunk, never waiting its turn in the oldest-first ordering)"* — **no cap** |
| FR-15 | *"`pinned` entries are refreshed in every Chunk and never wait their turn in the oldest-first ordering, **subject to the 25% cap of FR-17**"* — cap **subordinates the guarantee** |
| FR-17 row 1 | *"Every **`pinned`** entry, every Chunk, **without exception** \| this is what `pinned` means (FR-15)"* — guarantee is **absolute** |

Row 1 and FR-15 are reconcilable only under the load-time reading: the cap rejects an oversized tracked list, so at runtime every pinned entry really is refreshed without exception. FR-17's own enforcement clause supports that reading (*"enforced as a `data/tracked.json` validation error"*). But FR-15's *"subject to"* is the language of a runtime budget, and a builder reading FR-15 first will implement a per-Chunk truncation of the pinned set — which then makes FR-17 row 1's *"without exception"* false and makes `pinned` entries silently queue, the exact failure FR-17 says is *"indistinguishable from a slow refresh."* The Glossary, which §3 says downstream workflows use verbatim, mentions no cap at all.

**Fix shape:** FR-15 should read *"…and never wait their turn; the size of the pinned set is bounded at load (FR-17) so that this guarantee is always satisfiable"*, and the Glossary should carry the cap.

### F5 — Medium — FR-17's cap cites FR-16, which contains no cap

> FR-17: *"enforced as a `data/tracked.json` validation error rather than a convention **(FR-16)**"*

FR-16 is *"Reject an overlapping tracked list at load"* and, after C-4, defines exactly one thing: the overlap predicate. It says nothing about set sizes or budget. The citation is presumably meant as "the same load-time validation pass as FR-16", but the PRD's citation convention is *"the citation `(AD-n)` points at the decision and its rationale"*, and FR-x cites are used the same way throughout. A builder chasing the cap's authority into FR-16 finds nothing.

Related and worth a line: FR-16's own title and body are now about overlap only, while three separate load-time validations have accumulated across FRs (overlap in FR-16, shared floor in FR-16/FR-22, pinned-set size in FR-17, and FR-29's empty containment set — see F6). No FR owns "what `tracked.json` validation checks", which is the artifact a builder actually writes.

### F6 — Medium — FR-29's "validation error" has no enforcement home, and inverts FR-27's severity ordering

C-2's text landed intact:

> FR-29: *"A Modifier Reference whose containment set is **empty** under that scope is a **validation error**, not a `P = 0`."*

But nothing says which artifact this rejects or which package raises it. FR-27's hard-error list — rewritten wholesale by C-13 — does not include it. FR-16's load-time rejections do not include it. And it cannot be a pure `tracked.json` check, because it depends on the *weights file's* contents at the entry's floor, so it is a cross-artifact validation that neither FR owns.

The severity ordering is also inverted against its neighbours. Under FR-27 and FR-4, the **larger** failures are soft:

> FR-27: *"A `partial` pool, a Base Type absent entirely and a slot with an empty pool are **degraded but loadable** — the Base Type leaves the ordering rather than the file being refused."*

So a Base Type missing from the weights file entirely degrades to Unrankable, while a single tracked band that happens to be unrollable at its entry's floor is a hard error. FR-29 gives a good reason for the hard treatment (*"it means the curator is tracking a tier that cannot roll on the item being crafted, and contributing zero would hide that"*) — but the reason argues for a **loud, per-entry** failure, which is what `unresolvable` and the Sync Report already provide for the structurally identical FR-24 case (*"**Two checks, two surfaces**"*). FR-24 solved this problem; FR-29 does not reuse the solution.

### F7 — Low-medium — `absent` provenance appears in FR-11's banner test but by construction never appears in the ranking

FR-4 and FR-28 both claim exclusivity for the `absent` path, and both put it on rows that are *outside* the ordering:

> FR-4: *"Every probability derived from a `partial` pool carries Provenance `absent` and is rendered as an **unknown rather than a number**… **this is the only path by which `absent` arises**"*

> FR-28: *"`core` assigns such probabilities Provenance `absent` and the Base Type is Unrankable (FR-4) — this is the only path by which `absent` arises"*

FR-11 then tests the ranking's honesty using both values:

> FR-11: *"When **every** probability in the loaded set carries `uniform-prior` or `absent`, the view shows a **persistent, dismissible-per-session banner** stating that **the entire ranking** rests on a uniform prior."*

Since every `absent` probability belongs to an Unrankable Base Type, "the loaded set" and "the ranking" are different populations, and the banner's copy describes the second while its condition reads the first. The degenerate case is real and reachable — FR-30: *"Until a conforming file exists, **every Base Type is Unrankable**"* — and produces a banner asserting something about a ranking that is empty. It also means FR-11's self-clearing property (*"The banner **disappears on its own** once any `measured` figure is present"*) can be defeated by a single Unrankable row, since `absent` never upgrades.

**Fix shape:** scope FR-11's condition to probabilities backing **ranked** rows, i.e. `uniform-prior` only, and let FR-4 carry the Unrankable-coverage messaging it already owns via the AD-27 table.

### F8 — Low — `pinned` is used as a non-Glossary verb in FR-22

> FR-22: *"Raw Bases are **pinned** to item level 82 (FR-3)."*

§3 opens with *"Downstream readers and workflows use these terms exactly. Introducing a synonym anywhere is a discipline violation."* `pinned` is now a Curation Status value with schema-level behaviour across FR-15 and FR-17, so this sentence reads at a glance as though Raw Bases carry Curation Status `pinned`. Pre-existing wording, but the rev-2 edits are what made the collision load-bearing. Use "fixed at" or "always filter at".

## 4. Introduced without being asked for

None of these is wrong; all are worth a deliberate check against the spine before they harden.

- **FR-27 keeps four hard errors beyond C-13's list.** C-13 said *"Replace with the 2.0.0 list"* and named five. The PRD has nine — the five, plus *"an unknown `schemaVersion` major"*, *"overlapping value bands for one `statId` within a slot"*, *"a missing or negative `weight`"*, *"a missing `poolCoverage`"*. All four are pre-existing or plainly implied by schema 2.0.0, and dropping them would have been worse. Flagged only because "replace" became "extend" silently.
- **FR-33 names the four catalogue files.** The proposals say only *"four requests"* and *"`web` now reads two of the four"*. The PRD commits to `catalogue/stats.json`, `catalogue/static.json`, `catalogue/items.json`, `catalogue/filters.json`, and to which two the view fetches. Check the filenames against AD-25 — nothing in the proposals supplies them.
- **Two FR-17 bullets go beyond AD-26 as the proposals describe it.** *"`not-yet-synced` is treated as **infinitely old**, so a newly added entry is picked up before any refresh"* and *"**A resumed Chunk recomputes the order rather than replaying a frozen plan.** Progress records which entries a Chunk *completed*, not which it intended to visit."* Both are sound and both are necessary to make the rotation implementable; the second in particular interacts with FR-19's progress file and is the kind of thing the spine should own rather than the PRD.
- **FR-29's `(Base Type, slot)` scoping clause** — *"The `(Base Type, slot)` scoping is not optional and is the commonest way two implementations diverge"* — is not in C-2's proposed text. Clearly implied by AD-18 and an improvement.
