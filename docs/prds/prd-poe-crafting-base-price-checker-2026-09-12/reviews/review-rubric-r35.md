# PRD Quality Review — PoE2 Crafting Base Price Checker (revision 35)

Rubric: `bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`. Scope: the revision 35 delta only (UJ-7; §3 *Raw Base*, *Base Filter*, *Pick-up Filter*; §4.11 FR-35 to FR-37; the §6 non-goal *Combinations in the Pick-up Filter*; the §7.1 line; the §0 FR range), judged for fit against FR-1, FR-3, FR-4, FR-9, FR-10 to FR-12, FR-26, NFR-10 and §3.

The working-tree `prd.md` changed while this review ran. This review judges the later state: FR-35 cites AD-31 in place of the `[ASSUMPTION]`, FR-35 has the "dial change … reaches the game only when he saves the filter again" bullet, FR-36 has the two-floor bullet and the no-price bullet, and §10 lists OQ-28. AD-31 and OQ-28 in `ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md` were read as context only.

## Overall verdict

The Pick-up Filter is a well-shaped addition. UJ-7 is a real journey, FR-36 states its purpose rule as a predicate with its reason ("crafting on a base forfeits its raw sale"), and the §6 non-goal states the limit honestly. The delta keeps to altitude: AD-31 owns the mechanism, `EXPERIENCE.md` owns the treatments, and no field names or file paths came into the PRD. The risk is in the predicates. FR-36 does not cover every white base of a class worth crafting: a sub-threshold or unpriced Raw Base gets no purpose. FR-36 is undefined when the active Craft Recipe is uncostable (FR-26). FR-37 has a headline that no test can check, and it depends on a Base Filter that the tool never reads. These gaps are in the rules a story will be built from, so done-ness is the weak dimension.

## 1. Decision-readiness — adequate

The delta makes its decisions openly. FR-36's craft-versus-sell rule gives what crafting costs (the raw sale), FR-35 says that the filter is a snapshot and not a second judgement, and FR-37 says that NFR-10 does not bind the filter. Each decision is marked *(PRD-owned)*. The weak point is that two of these decisions give up something the PRD elsewhere calls essential, and the delta does not name that cost.

### Findings
- **[medium]** The snapshot rule gives up SM-C4 without saying so (§4.11 FR-35) — "A figure that FR-10 to FR-12 mark as weak or not current enters the filter unmarked; the player reads those marks in the view when he generates it." SM-C4 calls this exact failure, an estimated or old figure shown as cleanly as a measured one, the thing that makes the tool "more dangerous". The JTBD *Know when a figure cannot be trusted* says the same. The next bullet in the same FR makes it worse: the filter stays in the game until the player saves it again, so a filter that is weeks old shows its purposes with no sign of their age. UJ-7 does not include the step in which the player reads the marks. *Fix:* in FR-35, name this as a trade-off against SM-C4 and the trust JTBD, and say why it is accepted. Alternatively, add FR-35 to SM-C4's counterbalance and let `EXPERIENCE.md` decide whether the view warns at generation time.
- **[medium]** The NFR-10 exemption is decided in FR-37, not in NFR-10 (§4.11 FR-37, §5 NFR-10) — "NFR-10 binds the view, not the Pick-up Filter". NFR-10 has no scope limit ("Colour alone never carries a product-meaningful distinction"), and one of the distinctions it lists is "crafted versus uncrafted (FR-3)". That is the same split as craft versus sell raw. A reader of NFR-10 alone gets the wrong answer. The accessibility cost is also not stated as a cost. *Fix:* scope NFR-10 in NFR-10 itself ("in the view; FR-37 states the filter's bound") and remove the restatement from FR-37. Say what a colour-blind player loses on the ground.

## 2. Substance over theater — strong

Nothing in the delta is there for show. UJ-7 drives FR-35's "no network" and FR-37's "at a glance". The Description of §4.11 gives the problem in one sentence ("reads it before a session and then plays from memory for two hours"). The glossary entries each define a term that the FRs use. No findings.

## 3. Strategic coherence — adequate

The filter carries the Vision's thesis into the game. It is generated from the ranking under the dial, and the view stays the only place to read Combinations. The §6 rewrite drops "The web view is the permanent product, not a step toward another one" and replaces it with "the filter is generated by it and does not replace it". That keeps the thesis. But the PRD does not measure whether the filter does its job, or whether it pulls the player away from the view.

### Findings
- **[medium]** No success metric validates FR-35 to FR-37 (§8) — every primary SM lists the FRs it validates, and none lists FR-35 to FR-37. SM-2 ("The mental top-five goes away") is the natural owner. The thesis also has a new failure mode with no counter-metric: the player saves the filter and stops reading Chase Combinations, which leaves UJ-1 and FR-2 behind. *Fix:* add FR-35 to FR-37 to SM-2's "Validates" list. Consider a counter-metric, or a note on SM-1 or SM-2, for "the filter replaces the read".
- **[medium]** "Check the rolls" is a second judgement that FR-35 says the filter does not make (§4.11 FR-36, FR-35, §2.2, §6) — FR-35: "The filter is a snapshot of the ranking, not a second judgement of it." The ranking values a white base crafted under a Craft Recipe (FR-1, FR-26). It never values a magic drop that has already rolled, and the unidentified-tier 4-or-5 cut-off is a valuation that the ranking does not make. The purpose also comes close to §2.2's non-user "Players wanting advice on an item already in hand" and §6's augment non-goal, and neither one says where its boundary falls. *Fix:* say in FR-36 that check the rolls is a pick-up heuristic outside the EV model, and give the reason for it. In §6, say that picking up a magic drop to identify it is not advice on an item in hand.

## 4. Done-ness clarity — thin

FR-35 can be tested: no request, the current dial, no Combination, modifier or price, and the Base Filter precedence. FR-36 states its predicates in a form a test can use, and that is the right form for the PRD. But the predicates do not cover every case, they have one undefined state, and FR-37's headline cannot be tested.

### Findings
- **[high]** FR-36 hides white bases of a class worth crafting (§4.11 FR-36) — the two branches are "**craft** when EV(C) > 0 and price(B) < EV(C)" and "**sell raw** when price(B) ≥ EV(C) and price(B) ≥ Payout Threshold". A white B with EV(C) > 0 and EV(C) ≤ price(B) < Payout Threshold matches neither branch. The final bullet also drops every unpriced white Raw Base from craft: "A Raw Base with no price has no craft and no sell-raw entry, because both comparisons need its price". In both cases the class is worth crafting and its base does not show. This conflicts with the product's own valuation. A sub-threshold raw sale "is worth nothing to him" (§2.1, §4.2), and FR-3 removes such a base from the ordering, so crafting gives up nothing. The bases most likely to have no listings are the cheap ones, and those are the ones the player crafts on. The given reason is a mechanical one (the comparison needs a number), not a product trade-off. *Fix:* compare EV(C) against the raw value truncated at the threshold, which is price(B) when price(B) ≥ Payout Threshold and nothing otherwise. Then state the unpriced case as a product decision with its cost. For example: "an unpriced white Raw Base of a class with EV(C) > 0 is craft, because an unknown raw price is not a known forfeit; FR-9 still shows the price as unknown in the view".
- **[high]** FR-36 is undefined under an uncostable Craft Recipe (§4.11 FR-36, FR-35, FR-26) — FR-26 says that while the active recipe is uncostable "the player still sees each of FR-3's two ranked units in its own order, and no ordering between them". EV(C) then has no Craft Cost term, so "EV(C) > 0" and "price(B) < EV(C)" cannot be evaluated. FR-35's "shows exactly what the ranking says" gives no answer. This state happens when a currency has no rate yet (FR-23), which is most likely at league start, the moment R-6 calls the most valuable. Two builders will make different choices. *Fix:* add an FR-36 consequence for an uncostable recipe, citing FR-26. Options: generation is refused with the reason, or the filter carries sell raw only. Choose one and name it *(PRD-owned)*.
- **[high]** FR-37's headline cannot be tested, and its consequence needs a filter the tool never reads (§4.11 FR-37, §3 *Base Filter*) — "each one still reads the same if he goes back to his Base Filter alone" has no observable meaning that a test could check. "Each treatment reuses the meaning the Base Filter's own conventions give that kind of item" assumes that the Base Filter's conventions are known. But §3 defines the Base Filter as "the player's own third-party item filter … The tool neither reads nor edits it". So the conventions belong to one specific filter family that the PRD does not name. *Fix:* state the actual promise in the headline. For example: the three purposes look different from each other, and each looks like what the Base Filter's conventions would show for an item of that value. Record which Base Filter family's conventions apply, as an `[ASSUMPTION]` or as a pointer to `EXPERIENCE.md`, and do not restate the convention in the PRD.
- **[low]** "Quiet" is an adjective with no bound (§4.11 FR-37) — "The treatments are quiet: the filter exists to unhide and label, not to alarm". *Fix:* a citation is enough. Add "`EXPERIENCE.md` owns what quiet means" or state the promise as a comparison, for example that no treatment is louder than the Base Filter's treatment for the same tier of item. Do not add mechanism.

## 5. Scope honesty — adequate

The §6 non-goal is honest and gives its reason. §7.1 states the boundary ("never Combinations"). OQ-28 is listed in §10 with an owner. The `[ASSUMPTION]` in FR-35 was replaced by an AD-31 citation during this review, so the delta now has no open-item callouts. That is in proportion. Two scope consequences are left for the reader to infer.

### Findings
- **[medium]** A Raw Base now has two jobs, and the cost is not stated (§3 *Raw Base*, §4.11 FR-36, FR-1, SM-C1) — "The Raw Bases of an Item Class are also the bases of that class the player would craft on". So a class gets craft entries in the filter only through raw Tracked Entries, and each raw entry costs a search on every refresh (R-4, SM-C1). A class with crafted entries and no raw entry is absent from the filter, with no sign of that. FR-1's reason for valuing at class level is "the player crafts on whatever the class gives him", but the filter now limits crafting to the curated subset of Base Types. *Fix:* in FR-36, say that a class with no Raw Base has no filter entry, and cite SM-C1 for the budget cost. Add one sentence that connects the curated craft bases to FR-1's class-wide price spread: the filter steers the player toward the bases that FR-1 says the class figure understates.
- **[low]** Pruned Raw Bases are not excluded explicitly (§4.11 FR-36) — "The bases are the Raw Bases of the Tracked List". By §3, a Raw Base is any raw Tracked Entry, and that includes `pruned` tombstones. FR-35's "snapshot of the ranking" implies they are excluded, but FR-36 does not say so. *Fix:* "…the Raw Bases of the Tracked List that are not `pruned` (FR-15)".
- **[low]** The unidentified-tier fact has no tag (§4.11 FR-36) — "when the game shows its unidentified tier as 4 or 5, the two best". This is a game fact and a purpose depends on it. OQ-28 checks three in-game facts, and this is not one of them. *Fix:* tag it `[ASSUMPTION]`, or ask the spine to add it to OQ-28.

## 6. Downstream usability — adequate

The FR ids continue in sequence (FR-35 to FR-37, §0 updated). §7.1, §6 and §10 resolve to the new FRs and to OQ-28. AD-31 resolves in the spine and its *Prevents* list cites FR-35 and FR-36. Each new FR says what it realises. But the delta adds several domain nouns that §3 does not define, and §3 says "§3 defines every domain noun".

### Findings
- **[low]** "Dial", "jewel kind" and the three purposes are not in §3 (§1, §3 *Pick-up Filter*, §4.11, UJ-7) — In §1, "dial" means the threshold alone ("the threshold is a dial the player turns"). In FR-35, §3 and UJ-7, "the current dial" means the threshold plus the Craft Recipe (FR-35: "the Payout Threshold and Craft Recipe on screen"). "Jewel kind" is used four times and is not defined. FR-36 also says "a magic jewel of a kind whose EV is above zero", which gives EV to a "kind" when §3 gives EV to an Item Class or a Raw Base. Craft, sell raw and check the rolls appear in UJ-7, FR-36 and FR-37 as domain nouns. *Fix:* add **Dial** (Payout Threshold plus active Craft Recipe) and **Purpose** (the three values) to §3. Define a jewel kind in terms of Item Class or Base Type, or replace the term with one of those.
- **[low]** "Item it names" and "bases it names" are not the same thing (§4.11 FR-35, §3 *Pick-up Filter*) — FR-35: "For an item it names, its treatment is final and overrides the Base Filter". §3 and FR-36 say the filter names *bases*. An item of a named base that fails FR-36's conditions (below the floor, magic with tier 1 to 3, rare) would be "named" in one reading and not in the other. AD-31's block conditions settle it in favour of the narrow reading. *Fix:* in FR-35, write "an item that meets one purpose's conditions (FR-36)".
- **[low]** The rule is stated twice (§3 *Raw Base*, §4.11 FR-36) — the glossary entry states a business rule ("they are the bases the Pick-up Filter names … *(PRD-owned)*"), and FR-36's first bullet states it again ("The bases are the Raw Bases of the Tracked List"). *Fix:* keep the rule in FR-36. Reduce the glossary sentence to a definition that cites FR-36, without the *(PRD-owned)* mark.

## 7. Shape fit — strong

UJ-7 uses the same lighter journey form as UJ-1 to UJ-6, which suits a single operator. The feature is one §4 section with three FRs, in proportion to its scope. No findings.

## Mechanical notes

- **ID continuity.** FR-35 to FR-37 follow FR-34 with no gap. The §0 range says "FR-1 to FR-37". UJ-7 follows UJ-6. OQ-28 is listed in §10 with an owner and resolves in the spine.
- **Cross-references.** AD-31, AD-15, AD-24, NFR-6, FR-1, FR-3, FR-4, FR-9, FR-22 and AD-17 in §4.11 all resolve. The craft predicate's use of EV(C) is consistent with FR-1's "EV ≤ 0" case: a class whose priced Combinations are all below the threshold has EV = −Craft Cost, so it gets no craft entry. A jewel class with Craft Cost 0 (FR-26) and nothing above the threshold has EV = 0, so it gets no entry either. Both results match "above zero".
- **Wording against AD-31.** FR-35 says "The tool writes nothing outside the viewer's own browser storage (AD-15)", but the player saves a downloaded file. AD-31 and the spine's AD-15 revisit note treat that download as a permitted write path that amends AD-15. *Fix:* "The tool writes nothing itself; the player's download is the one exception AD-15 permits (AD-31)."
- **Altitude.** UJ-7's "saves a fresh Pick-up Filter over last session's and reloads it in the game" depends on AD-31's fixed filename and on the game's reload control. It is acceptable as journey narration, but it is the one place where the delta comes close to control mechanics. No field names, file paths or display strings came into the PRD with the delta.
- **Assumptions index.** The PRD has no Assumptions Index section. After the AD-31 citation, the delta has no inline `[ASSUMPTION]`. If the unidentified-tier fact or the Base Filter family is tagged (see §4 and §5 above), think about where the tags are listed.
- **Frontmatter.** `status: draft`, `revision: 35`, and the new source `docs/forge/loot-filter-integration/forged-idea.md` are consistent with an open revision. `updated: 2026-10-31` was not bumped.
